# SDS-PAGE: Principle and Procedure

## Introduction

**SDS-PAGE (Sodium Dodecyl Sulfate–Polyacrylamide Gel Electrophoresis)** is a widely used technique for separating proteins primarily according to their molecular mass.

It is commonly used to examine protein composition, estimate molecular weight and assess sample purity.

## Principle

Proteins naturally differ in size, shape and charge. SDS-PAGE minimizes the effect of these differences by treating proteins with **SDS**, a detergent that denatures proteins and gives them a broadly similar negative charge-to-mass ratio.

During electrophoresis, proteins migrate through a polyacrylamide gel toward the positive electrode.

Smaller proteins generally move through the gel more easily and therefore migrate farther than larger proteins.

Role of SDS

Sodium dodecyl sulfate (SDS) is an anionic detergent.

Its major functions are to:

Disrupt many non-covalent interactions
Denature proteins
Provide a strong negative charge
Reduce the influence of the protein's native charge and shape
Role of Reducing Agents

Reducing agents such as β-mercaptoethanol or DTT may be used when reducing conditions are required.

They break disulfide bonds, helping separate protein subunits that are connected by disulfide linkages.

Therefore:

SDS + reducing conditions → denatured protein chains

Polyacrylamide Gel

The gel acts as a molecular sieve.

It is commonly composed of two regions:

Stacking Gel

The stacking gel concentrates proteins into a relatively narrow starting zone before they enter the resolving gel.

Resolving Gel

The resolving gel is responsible for the major separation of proteins according to size.

Higher-percentage gels generally provide better separation of smaller proteins, while lower-percentage gels are more suitable for larger proteins.

Protein Ladder

A protein molecular-weight ladder contains proteins of known approximate molecular masses.

It is loaded alongside experimental samples and provides a reference for estimating the molecular mass of unknown protein bands.

General Procedure
1. Prepare Protein Samples

Extracted protein samples are mixed with an appropriate sample buffer containing SDS.

Reducing agents may be included when reducing SDS-PAGE is required.

2. Prepare the Gel System

A polyacrylamide gel is placed into the electrophoresis apparatus and the appropriate running buffer is added.

3. Load Samples

The protein ladder and prepared samples are carefully loaded into separate wells.

Careful loading helps prevent:

Cross-contamination
Sample loss
Uneven bands
4. Electrophoresis

An electric field is applied across the gel.

Because SDS-treated proteins carry a strong negative charge, they migrate toward the positive electrode.

Smaller proteins generally migrate faster through the gel matrix.

5. Staining

After electrophoresis, the gel can be stained using an appropriate protein stain, such as Coomassie Brilliant Blue, to visualize protein bands.

6. Destaining and Visualization

Excess stain is removed so that the protein bands become easier to observe and compare.

Applications

SDS-PAGE is used for:

Protein separation
Molecular-weight estimation
Protein purity assessment
Comparing protein expression
Checking protein extraction
Monitoring protein purification

Common Problems
Observation	Possible reason
No visible bands	Low protein amount or staining problem
Smearing	Overloading, degradation or poor sample preparation
Distorted bands	Uneven loading or electrophoresis problems
Poor separation	Inappropriate gel percentage or running conditions
Very faint bands	Insufficient protein or staining
Uneven migration	Buffer, gel or apparatus problem
Precautions
Prepare protein samples consistently.
Avoid overloading the gel.
Load samples carefully.
Use an appropriate protein ladder.
Ensure the electrophoresis system is assembled correctly.
Handle SDS and other reagents according to laboratory safety procedures.
Follow the validated laboratory protocol for electrophoresis conditions.

Key Takeaways
SDS-PAGE separates proteins mainly according to molecular mass.
SDS denatures proteins and gives them a strong negative charge.
Polyacrylamide acts as a molecular sieve.
Smaller proteins generally migrate farther through the gel.
A protein ladder provides a reference for molecular-weight estimation.
Coomassie Brilliant Blue is commonly used to visualize protein bands.
Learning Note

I understood SDS-PAGE more clearly when I connected the roles of SDS, the polyacrylamide gel and the electric field. The important idea is that SDS makes the proteins behave more similarly in terms of charge, allowing their migration through the gel to depend mainly on their size.
