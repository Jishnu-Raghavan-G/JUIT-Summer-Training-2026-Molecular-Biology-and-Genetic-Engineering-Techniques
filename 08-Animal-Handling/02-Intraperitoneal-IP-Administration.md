# Intraperitoneal (IP) Administration

## Introduction

**Intraperitoneal (IP) administration** is a method of delivering a substance into the peritoneal cavity of a laboratory animal.

The peritoneal cavity is the space within the abdomen surrounding many abdominal organs. IP administration is used in approved animal studies when this route is appropriate for the experimental purpose.

## Principle

The substance is introduced into the peritoneal cavity using an appropriate sterile technique and equipment.

Common Applications

IP administration may be used in research involving:

Pharmacological studies
Toxicological studies
Drug-response experiments
Experimental disease models
Administration of approved research substances

The route should only be used when scientifically justified and included in the approved animal protocol.

Important Considerations

Before administration, the researcher should confirm:

Correct animal identification
Correct substance and concentration
Appropriate administration route
Animal health and condition
Approved experimental protocol
Appropriate equipment
Required volume and dose according to the approved protocol
Animal Restraint

Proper restraint is important for both animal welfare and researcher safety.

The animal should be restrained in a manner that:

Provides adequate control
Minimizes unnecessary stress
Prevents sudden movement
Allows the procedure to be performed safely

Handling should be calm and consistent.

Administration Site

For IP administration, the appropriate abdominal region and anatomical landmarks must be identified according to the species-specific institutional protocol.

Correct positioning helps reduce the risk of accidental injury to internal organs.

General Procedure

The procedure should be performed only by trained personnel following the approved institutional protocol.

A general sequence is:

Confirm the animal's identity and health condition.
Verify the substance, dose and route.
Prepare the required sterile equipment.
Restrain the animal appropriately.
Identify the approved anatomical administration region.
Perform the administration using the institutionally approved technique.
Dispose of used sharps safely.
Observe the animal after administration.
Record the procedure and any relevant observations.
Post-Administration Observation

Animals should be observed after administration for abnormal signs such as:

Unusual movement
Excessive distress
Changes in breathing
Reduced activity
Bleeding
Swelling or other local abnormalities

Any concerning observation should be reported according to the approved animal-care procedure.

Common Sources of Error
Problem	Possible consequence
Incorrect animal identification	Wrong experimental assignment
Poor restraint	Increased stress or accidental injury
Incorrect administration site	Risk of tissue or organ injury
Incorrect substance or dose	Invalid experimental result or harm
Poor aseptic practice	Risk of infection
Inadequate observation	Delayed recognition of adverse effects
Safety and Ethics

IP administration must be performed:

By trained personnel
Under appropriate institutional supervision
Using approved procedures
With appropriate animal-welfare considerations
With suitable personal protective equipment

The procedure should minimize pain, distress and unnecessary handling.

Documentation

Important information should be recorded, including:

Animal identification
Date and time
Substance administered
Route
Dose or volume as specified in the approved protocol
Relevant observations
Any unexpected event

Accurate records are essential for reproducibility and animal welfare monitoring.

Key Takeaways
IP administration delivers a substance into the peritoneal cavity.
Correct animal identification and protocol verification are essential.
Appropriate restraint reduces stress and improves procedural safety.
The administration site must be selected using species-appropriate anatomical guidance.
Post-procedure observation is important for animal welfare.
Only trained personnel should perform IP administration under an approved protocol.
