# Intramuscular (IM) Administration

## Introduction

**Intramuscular (IM) administration** is a method of delivering an approved substance directly into skeletal muscle tissue.

It is used in laboratory animal studies when the intramuscular route is appropriate for the experimental objective and has been included in the approved animal protocol.

## Principle

The substance is delivered into an appropriate muscle using a suitable sterile technique.

Common Applications

IM administration may be used in approved studies involving:

Pharmacological research
Vaccine studies
Drug-response experiments
Physiological research
Experimental disease models

The route should be selected according to the scientific purpose and animal-welfare requirements.

Important Considerations

Before the procedure, the researcher should verify:

Correct animal identification
Animal health and condition
Correct substance
Appropriate dose and volume according to the approved protocol
Correct administration route
Suitable equipment
Institutional approval and supervision
Animal Restraint

Proper restraint helps prevent sudden movement and reduces the risk of injury.

The restraint method should:

Be appropriate for the species
Provide adequate control
Minimize unnecessary stress
Allow safe access to the selected muscle

Animals should be handled calmly and consistently.

Selection of Injection Site

The injection site depends on the species, animal size and approved experimental protocol.

The selected muscle should be appropriate for the required administration and should avoid major nerves, blood vessels and vulnerable anatomical structures.

Species-specific anatomical guidance must always be followed.

General Procedure

The procedure should be performed only by trained personnel using the approved institutional technique.

A general sequence is:

Confirm the animal's identity and condition.
Verify the substance, dose and route.
Prepare the required sterile equipment.
Restrain the animal appropriately.
Identify the approved muscle and administration site.
Perform the administration using the validated technique.
Dispose of used sharps safely.
Observe the animal after the procedure.
Record the procedure and relevant observations.
Post-Administration Observation

The animal should be observed for signs of an adverse response, including:

Excessive distress
Abnormal movement
Difficulty moving the affected limb
Bleeding
Swelling
Persistent pain or discomfort
Changes in activity or behavior

Any concerning observation should be reported according to the approved animal-care procedure.

Common Sources of Error
Problem	Possible consequence
Incorrect animal identification	Incorrect experimental assignment
Poor restraint	Increased stress or accidental injury
Incorrect injection site	Tissue, nerve or vascular injury
Incorrect substance or dose	Invalid result or potential harm
Poor aseptic practice	Risk of infection
Inadequate observation	Delayed detection of adverse effects
Safety and Ethics

IM administration must be carried out:

By trained personnel
Under appropriate supervision
According to an approved protocol
Using species-appropriate techniques
With appropriate protective equipment

The procedure should minimize pain, distress and unnecessary handling.

Documentation

The following information should be recorded:

Animal identification
Date and time
Substance administered
Route
Dose or volume specified by the approved protocol
Relevant observations
Any unexpected event

Accurate documentation supports both animal welfare and scientific reproducibility.

Key Takeaways
IM administration delivers a substance into skeletal muscle.
The correct animal, substance, dose and route must be verified before administration.
Proper restraint reduces stress and improves procedural safety.
The injection site must be selected using species-specific anatomical guidance.
Post-procedure observation is important for animal welfare.
Only trained personnel should perform IM administration under an approved protocol.
