# Good Laboratory Practices

## Introduction

Good Laboratory Practices (GLP) are the basic working habits and procedures that help maintain laboratory safety, accuracy, cleanliness, and reproducibility. In molecular biology, careful laboratory practice is important because small errors in measurement, sample handling, contamination control, or documentation can affect the final experimental result.

---

## 1. Personal Safety

Appropriate personal protective equipment (PPE) should be used according to the laboratory requirements.

Common PPE includes:

- Laboratory coat
- Gloves
- Safety glasses or other eye protection when required
- Closed footwear

Long hair should be secured, and loose clothing should be avoided around laboratory equipment.

---

## 2. Clean Working Area

A clean and organized workspace reduces contamination and accidents.

Good practices include:

- Clean the work surface before and after experiments.
- Keep only necessary materials on the bench.
- Keep reagent bottles closed when not in use.
- Clean spills immediately using the appropriate procedure.
- Keep clean and potentially contaminated materials separated.

---

## 3. Correct Labelling

Every sample and prepared reagent should be identifiable.

Labels may include:

- Sample or reagent name
- Concentration
- Date
- Storage condition
- Sample identification number
- Initials, where required

Proper labelling prevents accidental mixing or use of incorrect reagents.

---

## 4. Accurate Measurement

Accurate measurements are essential for reproducible experiments.

Important practices include:

- Use the appropriate micropipette for the required volume.
- Check the pipette setting before dispensing.
- Use calibrated balances for weighing reagents.
- Use appropriate measuring equipment for solution preparation.
- Check calculations before preparing solutions.

Even a small volume error can become significant in small reaction volumes.

---

## 5. Pipetting Practices

Good pipetting technique includes:

1. Selecting the correct pipette.
2. Using a suitable tip.
3. Setting the required volume accurately.
4. Aspirating smoothly.
5. Dispensing completely without excessive force.
6. Changing tips when required.
7. Avoiding contamination of the pipette and samples.

Pipetting should be performed consistently throughout an experiment.

---

## 6. Contamination Control

Contamination can affect DNA, RNA, protein, microbial, and PCR experiments.

To reduce contamination:

- Use clean or sterile materials as required.
- Change pipette tips between samples when necessary.
- Avoid touching sterile surfaces.
- Keep tubes and reagent containers closed.
- Separate samples when cross-contamination is possible.
- Keep PCR setup areas separate from areas where amplified products are handled when the laboratory workflow requires it.

---

## 7. Handling Chemicals

Chemical reagents should be handled according to their safety information and laboratory procedures.

Important practices include:

- Read the reagent label before use.
- Use appropriate PPE.
- Avoid direct contact with chemicals.
- Do not taste or intentionally inhale laboratory chemicals.
- Use a fume hood when required.
- Store chemicals according to their compatibility and storage requirements.

Chemical waste should be disposed of in the designated waste stream.

---

## 8. Biological Material

Biological samples and cultures should be handled according to the laboratory's biosafety procedures.

Important practices include:

- Use appropriate PPE.
- Avoid unnecessary exposure or splashing.
- Keep biological samples properly contained.
- Disinfect or decontaminate work areas according to laboratory procedures.
- Dispose of biological waste in designated containers.

---

## 9. Sharps Safety

Needles, blades, broken glass, and other sharp objects can cause injuries.

Good practices include:

- Handle sharps carefully.
- Never leave a sharp object loose on the bench.
- Do not dispose of sharps in ordinary waste.
- Use designated sharps containers.
- Follow the laboratory's procedure for accidental injuries.

---

## 10. Centrifuge Safety

Before centrifugation:

- Inspect tubes for damage.
- Use compatible tubes.
- Balance the centrifuge properly.
- Check the rotor and tube holders.
- Confirm the required centrifugation conditions.

During operation:

- Keep the lid closed.
- Do not attempt to stop the rotor manually.

After operation:

- Wait until the rotor has completely stopped before opening the lid.

---

## 11. Equipment Handling

Laboratory instruments should be operated according to their instructions or laboratory SOPs.

Before use:

- Check the instrument condition.
- Confirm required settings.
- Check calibration where applicable.
- Ensure the correct accessories are being used.

After use:

- Clean the equipment when required.
- Return components to their proper location.
- Switch off equipment when the procedure requires it.
- Report malfunctions rather than attempting unauthorized repairs.

---

## 12. Sample Identification and Traceability

Samples should remain identifiable throughout the entire experiment.

A simple workflow is:

       ↓

       ↓
Processing
       ↓
Storage
       ↓
Experiment
       ↓

       ↓
Documentation

A clear identification system reduces the risk of confusing samples or results.

13. Documentation

Laboratory work should be recorded clearly and promptly.

Useful information includes:

Date of experiment
Experiment title
Sample identification
Reagents used
Concentrations
Instrument settings
Important observations
Results
Deviations from the planned procedure
Any problems encountered

Good documentation makes it possible to understand and reproduce the experiment later.

14. Waste Disposal

Laboratory waste should be separated according to the type of waste and the laboratory's disposal procedures.

Examples include:

General laboratory waste
Biological waste
Sharps
Chemical waste
Contaminated disposable materials

Waste should never be disposed of in an inappropriate container simply for convenience.

15. Preventing Experimental Errors

Common causes of laboratory errors include:

incorrect calculations
wrong reagent concentration
incorrect pipette setting
poor sample labelling
contamination
incorrect incubation conditions
improper centrifugation
failure to follow the experimental protocol

A useful habit is to pause and verify critical steps before proceeding.

16. Reagent Storage

Reagents should be stored according to their specific requirements.

Important factors may include:

temperature
light exposure
humidity
container type
sterility
expiry or preparation date

Repeated unnecessary freeze-thaw cycles should be avoided for reagents that are sensitive to them.

17. Good Laboratory Communication

Laboratory work often involves multiple people using the same equipment and reagents.

Good communication includes:

reporting equipment problems
informing others about unusual observations
clearly identifying prepared solutions
following shared laboratory procedures
documenting changes to an experiment
asking for clarification when an unfamiliar procedure is involved
18. Common Mistakes to Avoid
Working without checking the protocol

This can lead to incorrect reagent volumes or experimental conditions.

Poor labelling

Unlabelled samples can become unusable or lead to incorrect conclusions.

Using contaminated materials

Contamination may not always be visible but can significantly affect results.

Ignoring equipment limitations

Every instrument has specified operating ranges and conditions.

Leaving the workspace disorganized

Poor organization increases the chance of spills, contamination, and sample mix-ups.

Failing to record observations

Important experimental information can easily be forgotten if it is not documented during the work.

Key Takeaways
Good laboratory practice improves safety, accuracy, and reproducibility.
Clean working conditions and contamination control are essential in molecular biology.
Accurate pipetting, weighing, labelling, and documentation reduce experimental errors.
Equipment should be operated within its specified limits.
Chemicals, biological materials, and laboratory waste must be handled according to appropriate safety procedures.
Proper documentation allows experimental work to be understood and reproduced later.
