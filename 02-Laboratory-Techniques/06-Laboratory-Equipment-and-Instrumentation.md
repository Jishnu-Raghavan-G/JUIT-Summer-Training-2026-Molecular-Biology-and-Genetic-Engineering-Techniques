# Laboratory Equipment and Instrumentation

## Introduction

Molecular biology laboratories use a variety of instruments to perform measurements, sample preparation, separation, amplification, and analysis. Understanding the basic function and correct handling of each instrument is important for obtaining reliable results and preventing equipment damage.

During laboratory training, I became familiar with several instruments commonly used in molecular biology workflows.

---

## 1. Micropipette

A micropipette is used to accurately measure and transfer small volumes of liquids, usually in the microlitre range.

Common ranges include:

| Pipette | Approximate Volume Range |
|---|---:|
| P10 | 0.5–10 µL |
| P20 | 2–20 µL |
| P200 | 20–200 µL |
| P1000 | 100–1000 µL |

Important practices include:

- selecting the correct pipette for the required volume
- using an appropriate disposable tip
- avoiding volume settings outside the pipette's specified range
- aspirating and dispensing smoothly
- changing tips between samples when required

---

## 2. Microcentrifuge

A microcentrifuge is used to separate components of small-volume samples using centrifugal force.

Common applications include:

- collecting cell pellets
- separating debris
- clarifying extracts
- collecting droplets at the bottom of tubes
- recovering sample fractions

Samples must be properly balanced before centrifugation.

---

## 3. Vortex Mixer

A vortex mixer is used for rapid mixing of liquid samples.

It is commonly used to:

- mix reagents
- resuspend pellets
- homogenize small samples
- mix reaction components

Sensitive samples may require gentle mixing instead of vigorous vortexing.

---

## 4. pH Meter

A pH meter measures the hydrogen-ion activity of a solution and provides a numerical indication of its acidity or alkalinity.

It is commonly used during:

- buffer preparation
- media preparation
- biochemical experiments

Basic handling involves:

1. Calibrating the instrument using appropriate standard buffers.
2. Rinsing the electrode as required.
3. Immersing the electrode in the sample.
4. Waiting for a stable reading.
5. Recording the pH.
6. Cleaning and storing the electrode according to the manufacturer's instructions.

The electrode should be handled carefully because it is a sensitive component.

---

## 5. Analytical Balance

An analytical balance is used to accurately measure the mass of laboratory reagents.

It is particularly useful when preparing:

- buffers
- culture media
- chemical solutions
- agarose gels
- protein assay reagents

Important practices:

- place the balance on a stable surface
- use a suitable weighing container
- avoid weighing directly on the balance pan
- allow the reading to stabilize
- keep the balance clean
- close the balance enclosure when applicable

---

## 6. Magnetic Stirrer

A magnetic stirrer uses a rotating magnetic field to mix liquids.

A small magnetic bar, called a **stir bar**, is placed inside the solution. The rotating field causes the bar to move and mix the liquid.

It is useful for:

- dissolving reagents
- preparing buffers
- mixing solutions uniformly

Excessive stirring should be avoided if it causes splashing or excessive foaming.

---

## 7. Water Bath

A water bath maintains samples at a controlled temperature using heated water.

It can be used for:

- incubation
- controlled heating
- warming reagents
- temperature-dependent laboratory procedures

Important considerations include:

- setting the required temperature
- ensuring sufficient water level
- avoiding contamination
- using appropriate tubes or containers

---

## 8. Heating Block

A heating block provides controlled heating of samples placed in suitable wells or blocks.

Compared with a water bath, it allows direct contact between the sample tube and the heated block.

Applications include:

- incubation of reaction mixtures
- controlled heating of samples
- enzyme-related procedures
- sample preparation

---

## 9. Refrigerator and Freezer

Refrigerators and freezers are used to store biological samples, reagents, enzymes, and prepared solutions.

Different materials require different storage temperatures.

Examples include:

- refrigerated storage for temperature-sensitive reagents
- frozen storage for many biological samples and enzymes
- deep-freezer storage for materials requiring lower temperatures

Storage conditions should always follow the specific reagent or sample requirements.

---

11. Gel Electrophoresis Apparatus

Gel electrophoresis equipment is used to separate nucleic acids or proteins according to their physical properties.

For DNA agarose gel electrophoresis, the basic setup includes:

electrophoresis tank
gel tray
comb
electrodes
power supply
agarose gel
running buffer

DNA samples are loaded into wells and separated when an electric field is applied.

12. Gel Documentation System

A gel documentation system is used to visualize and record bands present in electrophoresis gels.

It generally consists of:

imaging chamber
appropriate illumination system
camera
image acquisition software

It allows the gel image to be stored for analysis and documentation.

13. UV-Visible Spectrophotometer

A UV-Visible spectrophotometer measures the absorption of light by a sample at selected wavelengths.

It can be used for:

nucleic-acid quantification
protein assays
measuring absorbance of biological samples

For nucleic acids, absorbance around 260 nm is commonly used for concentration estimation.

The instrument should be blanked using the appropriate solvent or buffer before measuring samples.

14. Microplate Reader

A microplate reader measures signals such as absorbance, fluorescence, or luminescence from samples placed in multi-well plates.

It is useful for:

biochemical assays
protein quantification
enzyme assays
high-throughput measurements

The appropriate detection mode depends on the assay being performed.

15. SDS-PAGE Electrophoresis System

An SDS-PAGE system is used to separate proteins primarily according to their molecular size.

The basic system includes:

gel casting apparatus
polyacrylamide gel
electrophoresis chamber
electrodes
power supply

Protein samples migrate through the gel when an electric field is applied.

16. pH Meter vs Spectrophotometer

These instruments perform very different measurements:

Instrument	Main Measurement
pH meter	pH of a solution
Spectrophotometer	Light absorbance
Microplate reader	Absorbance/fluorescence/luminescence
Analytical balance	Mass
Centrifuge	Separation using centrifugal force
Thermal cycler	Programmed temperature cycling
17. General Instrumentation Practices

Before using an instrument:

Understand its purpose.
Check whether it is suitable for the experiment.
Inspect the instrument for obvious problems.
Confirm the required settings.
Follow the laboratory SOP or manufacturer's instructions.

After use:

clean the working area
return settings to the appropriate state when required
remove samples
dispose of waste correctly
report equipment problems to the responsible person
18. Calibration and Accuracy

Some instruments require periodic calibration to maintain reliable measurements.

Examples include:

micropipettes
analytical balances
pH meters
spectrophotometers
temperature-controlled equipment

Calibration helps ensure that the value measured by an instrument corresponds closely to the actual value.

19. Common Instrument-Related Errors
Incorrect settings

Using an incorrect temperature, speed, wavelength, or volume can affect the experiment.

Poor maintenance

Dirty or poorly maintained equipment can produce unreliable results.

Incorrect sample containers

Some instruments require specific tubes, cuvettes, or plates.

Ignoring calibration

Measurements from instruments that require calibration may become unreliable if calibration is not maintained.

Improper handling

Rough handling can damage sensitive components such as pipette mechanisms, pH electrodes, and optical systems.

Practical Checklist
 Instrument selected according to the experiment.
 Required settings verified.
 Calibration status checked when applicable.
 Correct sample container used.
 Instrument operated according to laboratory instructions.
 Samples removed after use.
 Instrument and surrounding area cleaned.
 Any malfunction reported.
Key Takeaways
Laboratory instruments perform specific functions within molecular biology workflows.
Correct settings and proper handling are essential for reliable results.
Calibration is important for instruments that provide quantitative measurements.
Equipment should be cleaned and maintained after use.
Understanding the purpose and limitations of each instrument helps prevent experimental errors.
Learning Note

During the training, I learned that laboratory instruments are not simply machines used to complete individual steps. Each instrument contributes to a particular stage of the experimental workflow, and even a small error in settings, calibration, or handling can affect the final result.
