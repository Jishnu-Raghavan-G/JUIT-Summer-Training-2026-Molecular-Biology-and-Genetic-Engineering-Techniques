# Agarose Gel Electrophoresis

## Introduction

Agarose gel electrophoresis is a laboratory technique used to separate DNA fragments according to their size. It is commonly used after DNA isolation, restriction digestion, PCR amplification, and other molecular biology experiments.

The technique allows DNA samples to be visualized as distinct bands and compared with a DNA ladder to estimate fragment sizes.

## Principle

DNA molecules have a negatively charged phosphate backbone. When an electric field is applied across an agarose gel, DNA migrates towards the **positive electrode (anode)**.

Smaller DNA fragments move through the pores of the agarose gel more easily and therefore migrate faster and farther than larger fragments.

Preparation of Agarose Gel

Agarose is dissolved in an appropriate electrophoresis buffer by heating.

For example, to prepare a 1% agarose gel in 50 mL:

Mass of agarose = 1 g/100 mL × 50 mL = 0.5 g

Therefore, 0.5 g agarose is required for 50 mL of buffer.

The agarose solution is allowed to cool sufficiently before adding any heat-sensitive staining reagent, if applicable. The exact staining method depends on the laboratory protocol.

Casting the Gel
Prepare the required agarose concentration.
Assemble the gel casting tray.
Place the comb to create wells.
Pour the molten agarose into the tray.
Allow the gel to solidify.
Carefully remove the comb.
Place the gel in the electrophoresis chamber with the appropriate buffer.

The wells should be positioned near the negative electrode, so that DNA migrates through the gel towards the positive electrode.

DNA Loading Dye

DNA loading dye is mixed with the sample before loading.

It helps to:

Increase the density of the sample so it settles into the well.
Make the sample easier to see during loading.
Provide tracking dyes that indicate the progress of electrophoresis.
DNA Ladder

A DNA ladder contains DNA fragments of known sizes.

It is loaded alongside the samples and acts as a reference for estimating the sizes of unknown DNA fragments.

For example:

DNA Ladder        Sample

1000 bp ──        ──
 700 bp ──
 500 bp ──        ──
 300 bp ──        ──
 100 bp ──

The sample bands can be compared with the ladder to obtain an approximate fragment size.

Loading the Gel
Mix the DNA sample with loading dye if required.
Place the pipette tip carefully into the well without damaging the gel.
Slowly dispense the sample.
Load the DNA ladder into a separate well.
Avoid introducing air bubbles into the wells.

Careful pipetting is particularly important because the wells can be easily damaged.

Electrophoresis Procedure
Place the prepared agarose gel in the electrophoresis tank.
Add sufficient running buffer to cover the gel.
Load the DNA ladder and samples into the wells.
Connect the electrodes correctly.
Apply the recommended voltage.
Allow DNA to migrate through the gel.
Stop electrophoresis when adequate separation has been achieved.
Visualize the DNA bands using the appropriate detection system.

The voltage and running time depend on the gel size, agarose concentration, DNA fragments and laboratory protocol.

Factors Affecting DNA Migration
1. DNA Fragment Size

Smaller fragments generally migrate faster than larger fragments.

2. Agarose Concentration

The concentration of agarose affects the pore size of the gel.

Agarose concentration	General use
Lower concentration	Better for larger DNA fragments
Higher concentration	Better for smaller DNA fragments
3. Applied Voltage

Higher voltage can increase migration speed but may reduce resolution or cause excessive heating if inappropriate.

4. DNA Structure

Linear, circular and supercoiled DNA can migrate differently even when their molecular masses are similar.

5. Running Buffer

The composition and concentration of the electrophoresis buffer affect conductivity and DNA migration.

Visualization of DNA

After electrophoresis, DNA bands are visualized using a suitable DNA stain and imaging system.

Depending on the staining method, DNA may appear as visible fluorescent bands under the appropriate illumination.

The gel documentation system can be used to capture and record the band pattern.

Interpretation of Results
Single Clear Band

A single clear band may indicate that the sample contains predominantly one DNA fragment of a particular size.

For PCR products, this may indicate amplification of the expected product.

Multiple Bands

Multiple bands may indicate:

More than one DNA fragment
Non-specific PCR amplification
Different restriction fragments
Contamination
Different DNA forms

The interpretation depends on the experiment.

Smear

A smear rather than distinct bands can result from:

Degraded DNA
Excessive DNA loading
Poor sample preparation
Inappropriate electrophoresis conditions
Overheating
Common Problems and Troubleshooting
Problem	Possible Cause
No bands visible	DNA absent, staining/detection problem, or loading error
Smearing	DNA degradation or excessive loading
Faint bands	Low DNA concentration
Distorted bands	Poor gel preparation or uneven running conditions
Sample leaks from well	Damaged well or incorrect loading
Poor separation	Incorrect agarose concentration or running conditions
Unexpected bands	Contamination or multiple DNA fragments
Precautions
Prepare the agarose gel carefully.
Make sure the gel is completely solidified before loading.
Do not damage the wells while pipetting.
Load samples slowly.
Avoid overloading DNA.
Ensure the electrodes are connected correctly.
Use the appropriate electrophoresis buffer.
Follow the recommended voltage and running time.
Handle DNA stains and visualization equipment according to laboratory safety procedures.
Applications

Agarose gel electrophoresis is used for:

Checking genomic DNA quality
Analysing PCR products
Analysing restriction digestion products
Estimating DNA fragment size
Checking DNA purification
Comparing DNA samples
Confirming the presence of specific DNA fragments

Key Takeaways
Agarose gel electrophoresis separates DNA mainly according to fragment size.
DNA moves towards the positive electrode because of its negative charge.
Smaller DNA fragments generally migrate farther through the gel.
Agarose concentration affects the separation range and resolution.
A DNA ladder is used to estimate fragment sizes.
The resulting band pattern provides useful information about the DNA sample.
