# DNA Restriction Enzyme Digestion

## Introduction

Restriction enzyme digestion is a molecular biology technique used to cut DNA at specific nucleotide sequences. Restriction enzymes, also called restriction endonucleases, recognize particular DNA sequences and cleave the DNA at or near those sites.

The technique is widely used in recombinant DNA technology, gene cloning, plasmid analysis, DNA mapping, and preparation of DNA fragments for ligation.

## Principle

Restriction enzymes recognize specific DNA sequences known as **restriction sites**. Many restriction sites are palindromic sequences.

For example, **EcoRI** recognizes:

5′ — GAATTC — 3′  
3′ — CTTAAG — 5′

EcoRI cuts between G and A, producing a short single-stranded overhang.

## Types of DNA Ends

### Sticky Ends

Sticky ends contain short single-stranded overhangs. These ends can base-pair with complementary DNA ends produced by the same or a compatible restriction enzyme.

### Blunt Ends

Blunt ends have no single-stranded overhang.

| Type | Description |
|---|---|
| Sticky end | Contains a single-stranded overhang |
| Blunt end | No overhang is present |

Sticky ends are particularly useful in cloning because complementary ends can temporarily pair before DNA ligation.

## Common Restriction Enzymes

| Enzyme | Recognition Sequence | End Produced |
|---|---|---|
| EcoRI | GAATTC | Sticky |
| HindIII | AAGCTT | Sticky |
| BamHI | GGATCC | Sticky |
| SmaI | CCCGGG | Blunt |

The exact reaction conditions depend on the enzyme and the manufacturer's recommendations.

## Components of a Restriction Digestion

A typical reaction contains:

- DNA sample
- Restriction enzyme
- Appropriate reaction buffer
- Nuclease-free water

The amount of DNA, enzyme and buffer should be selected according to the experimental protocol.

## General Procedure

1. Determine the amount and concentration of DNA.
2. Select the appropriate restriction enzyme.
3. Select the compatible reaction buffer.
4. Calculate the required volumes of all components.
5. Add the components using clean pipetting technique.
6. Mix gently and briefly collect the reaction at the bottom of the tube if required.
7. Incubate at the temperature and for the time recommended for the enzyme.
8. Heat-inactivate the enzyme if the enzyme and protocol permit it.
9. Analyse the digested DNA using agarose gel electrophoresis.

A typical setup is:

DNA + restriction enzyme + reaction buffer + nuclease-free water → incubation → digested DNA

## Role of the Restriction Buffer

The restriction buffer provides the appropriate chemical conditions for enzyme activity. It helps maintain suitable pH, ionic strength and cofactors required by the restriction enzyme.

Using the correct buffer is important because different enzymes can have different buffer requirements.

## Complete and Partial Digestion

### Complete Digestion

In complete digestion, the available recognition sites are efficiently cleaved under the reaction conditions.

### Partial Digestion

In partial digestion, only some of the available sites are cleaved.

Possible causes include:

- Insufficient enzyme
- Inadequate incubation time
- Unsuitable reaction conditions
- Poor DNA quality
- Difficult accessibility of restriction sites

Partial digestion can also be intentionally used in some DNA analysis procedures.

## Double Digestion

When two different restriction enzymes are used to digest the same DNA sample, the process is called **double digestion**.

Before performing a double digestion, it is necessary to check whether both enzymes work efficiently under compatible conditions, particularly with respect to buffer and incubation temperature.

## Star Activity

**Star activity** is the unwanted cleavage of DNA at sequences that are similar, but not identical, to the normal recognition site of a restriction enzyme.

It can be promoted by unsuitable reaction conditions, excessive enzyme concentration, or prolonged incubation.

Following the recommended reaction conditions helps minimize this problem.

## Analysis by Agarose Gel Electrophoresis

After digestion, the DNA fragments can be separated according to size using agarose gel electrophoresis.

The basic workflow is:

DNA sample  
↓  
Restriction digestion  
↓  
DNA fragments  
↓  
Agarose gel electrophoresis  
↓  
Band pattern  
↓  
Comparison with DNA ladder

The observed bands can be compared with the expected fragment sizes.

## Example

If a linear DNA molecule contains two restriction sites for an enzyme, complete digestion will generally produce three DNA fragments.

Controls

An undigested DNA sample is often useful as a comparison with the digested sample.

A DNA ladder is used to estimate the sizes of the resulting fragments.

Precautions
Store restriction enzymes according to the manufacturer's instructions.
Avoid unnecessary freeze-thaw cycles.
Use the buffer recommended for the enzyme.
Keep the reaction components clean.
Use fresh, appropriate pipette tips.
Handle enzymes carefully.
Follow the recommended incubation conditions.
Include suitable controls when required.

Restriction enzymes cut DNA at specific recognition sequences.
